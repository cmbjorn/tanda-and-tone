The player

Made for a milonga

Tandas and cortinas

Select four tracks and add them as a tanda, and a cortina follows it. Or lay out the night from a template (tango, tango, vals, tango, tango, milonga) and drop the tracks into its slots.

Up Next shows every tanda with its orchestra and the time it ends.

The room and your headphones

The bar along the top shows what the room hears: orchestra, singer and year, the time left in large digits, the tanda you are in and when it ends. Pre-listen plays any track on headphones while the room plays on, at about the level the room will give it. The room is amber, the headphones blue.

A cortina of a set length, the same pause

A cortina plays for the time you set, 30 seconds to start with, and fades out. Between two tracks the pause is the length you choose, whatever silence the files carry at their ends. Fade out ends a track or a cortina early.

A screen for the dancers

A second window for a projector or a TV: orchestra, title, singer and year in type that reads across the room, and where the tanda is. Your name and the milonga's stand at the top. What it says about the next tanda is up to you.

The lock

Locked, Stop and Next need a short hold, so a stray click cuts nothing short.

The end of the night

End after this tanda lets it play out and holds the room quiet. Export Tonight saves the set as a text list and a playlist.

Slower for class

Any track from 70 to 110 % of its tempo, with the pitch kept.

The sound

Every track at the right level

A Canaro from 1935 and a Di Sarli from 1957 are not at the same level in anyone's library. That difference comes from the transfers, not from the music, and Tanda & Tone takes it away before the track plays.

Between tracks

Four tracks of one tanda, from four transfers

An illustration of the idea, not a measurement.

As the files are

As the room hears them

Each track is set from the whole recording before it plays, so nothing hunts for the level while the music runs.

Inside a track

The touch is light. A soft passage comes up a few dB so it carries over a full floor; a build still builds, a pause stays silent and the last chords land at full weight.

Worn transfers

Clicks, crackle and hiss are cleaned as much as each recording needs, and a clean transfer is left alone. For the worst, the Restore Workshop makes new lossless copies ahead of the night. Your originals never change.

The PA

A limiter catches the peaks that would hurt the speakers, and nothing else.

Your settings

How it should sound is still your call

Leveling sets how much, and Soft is the lightest touch. Quiet lift sets how far a soft passage may come up, down to nothing. Room, Bass, Mid and Treble fit the hall, Restore is Off, Auto or On, and one track can go a little up or down, remembered for its next play. The volume is your mixer's.

For those who know compressors. Tango DJs who write about sound tend to describe the same ideal: level the library first, then a soft, slow compressor that takes a few dB off the loudest passages, with the bass kept out of what it listens to. Tanda & Tone is built that way.

The library

Your library, read where it is

Point Tanda & Tone at your music folders. Nothing is imported into a new format and nothing is retagged; it reads the files where they are and listens through each track once, so its level is known before you play it.

Orchestra, singer, rhythm, year

Read from your tags, FLAC included. Where the tags are thin, it takes what it can from the artist and the title, and a built-in catalogue of the golden-age orchestras gives a short name in full, and a singer or a date where it knows one for certain. Point at a value to see where it came from.

A tanda in a few clicks

Search and filter by orchestra, singer, rhythm and year, or show what you have not played for a month. Not sure who sings? Right-click a track to look it up on tango.info or El Recodo.

The best transfer

Where you own the same recording from several albums, the one that sounds best is marked. Which one plays is your choice.

Saved tandas and hints

A tanda you want again is saved with one click and waits in the sidebar. A small mark shows when a tanda mixes orchestras, singers or rhythms: a hint, never a block.

Never changed, not locked in

Corrections stay in the app, and nothing is written to your files. Retag in your own tagger whenever you like: the track keeps its playlists, tandas and history. Playlists go in and out as M3U.

Private

It works offline, in a hall with no signal. Nothing is recorded or uploaded. No account, no analytics, no tracking.

Questions

Good to know

More in the support guide.

What do I need?

A Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 14.2 or later, your music as files, and a cable from the Mac's headphone jack to the mixer. For pre-listen, headphones on a device of their own: a USB headphone adapter or Bluetooth headphones.

Does it play Spotify or other streaming services?

No. Tanda & Tone plays your own music files: MP3, AAC and Apple Lossless, WAV, AIFF and FLAC.

What happens to the dynamics?

They stay. The big correction is between tracks, where the difference comes from the transfers and not from the music. Inside a track the touch is light. If you want less, turn Leveling towards Soft, or set Quiet lift to zero.

How is this different from normalising my files?

Normalising gives each file one level, and that is the first thing Tanda & Tone does too. It then does what one number per file cannot: a gentle ride inside the track so soft passages carry in a full room, cleanup of clicks and crackle, the same pause between tracks and a limiter for the PA. And nothing is written to your files.

What if the Mac stops in the middle of a milonga?

The room goes quiet, because Tanda & Tone is the player. Open it again and it offers to go on from the same track with the same Up Next. As with any setup, keep a reserve on its own mixer channel, such as a phone with a playlist.
