Tanda & Tone

Keep your library. Keep the dynamics. Let go of the fader.

A Mac player for tango DJs that looks after the sound while you look after the floor. It matches the level from one transfer to the next and rides it gently inside a track, like a careful hand on the fader. The builds, the accents and the pauses stay as the orchestra played them.

The sound

The level is looked after. The dynamics are the orchestra's.

Tango lives on contrast: a whispered violin, a long build, a held pause before the last two chords. Tanda & Tone is made to keep all of it. What it takes away is the jump in level from one transfer to the next, and your reach for the fader.

As the files are

As the room hears them

This is where the work is

A quiet transfer from the thirties, a louder reissue, a modern orchestra: in one evening the files can differ by 10 dB from one track to the next. That difference is not music. It is how each transfer was made.

The room hears one level

Each track is set from the whole recording before it plays, so nothing hunts for the level while the music runs. One setting on your mixer holds for the evening.

Inside a track

A light touch, so the whole piece carries over a full floor

As recorded In the room

A milonga is not a quiet room. With a full floor the softest passages sink under the talk and the shoes, and the loudest are what make someone ask you to turn it down. A small, slow correction keeps the whole piece inside what the room can carry.

An illustration of the idea, not a measurement of one recording.

A whisper stays a whisper

It comes up by a few dB, enough to be heard over a full floor. You can set that lift lower, or switch it off.

A build still builds

A long crescendo keeps its climb, and the tutti still arrives as an event.

A pause stays silent

Nothing is filled in, and nothing swells while the orchestra holds its breath.

The last chord lands

After the pause, the final chords come at the full weight of the tutti.

If you know your compressors. Tango DJs who write about sound tend to describe the same ideal: level the library first, then a soft, slow compressor that takes a few dB off the loudest passages and is almost unnoticeable, with the bass kept out of what it listens to. That is how Tanda & Tone is built. Each track is levelled before it plays, so little is left for the compressor to do; the double bass and the left hands do not make the music pump; and the limiter at the end only catches the peaks that would hurt the PA. Leveling sets how much of all this you want, from Soft to Strict.

At a milonga

What it does for you during the night

Everything below happens while you play. None of it asks for your attention.

The sound

One setting on the mixer, all night

  • Every tanda at the right level. A 1938 transfer and a modern orchestra stand side by side, and you do not touch the fader.
  • The dynamics stay. Soft passages carry over a full floor and the tuttis stay comfortable, with the shape of the music kept.
  • Worn transfers cleaned up, gently. Clicks, crackle and hiss are taken down while you play. Clean transfers are left alone, and Off is one click.

The evening

Tandas and cortinas, laid out

  • Up Next in tandas. Tandas show as groups with a cortina between them and the time each one ends. A small mark hints at a tanda that mixes orchestras or rhythms; it never stops you.
  • Cortinas for a set length. A cortina plays for the time you choose, such as 30 seconds, and fades out.
  • The same pause between tracks. The gap is the length you set, whatever silence the files carry at their ends.

Peace of mind

Nothing cut short by accident

  • A lock against a wrong click. Locked, Stop and Next need a short hold.
  • The PA is guarded. A limiter catches the peaks that would hurt the speakers, and nothing else.
  • It picks up where it was. If the app stops, open it again and go on from the same track with the same Up Next.

The player

Made for running a milonga

What is playing and how much is left, the next tanda and when the set ends, your library in the middle. Nothing to learn that a tango DJ does not already do.

Now playing

Orchestra, singer and year under the title, time left in large digits, and which tanda you are in and when it ends.

Pre-listen

Hear the next track in your headphones while the room plays on, at about the level the room will give it.

Tone

Bass, Mid and Treble, up to 6 dB either way, for the touch you want in the room. Flat when you start.

Fade out

End a cortina or a track early, softly, when the floor asks for it.

Slower for class

Any track from 70 to 110 % of its tempo with the pitch kept, to teach a figure at an easier speed.

The set, afterwards

Every track you played, with its time, as a text list and a playlist file, ready to share after the night.

Your library

Your library stays yours

You do not import anything into a new format, and you do not retag. Tanda & Tone reads the music where it already is.

Finding the music

  • Tags read from your files, FLAC included.
  • Orchestra, singer and rhythm (tango, vals, milonga) as columns, with search and filters, so a tanda is a few clicks.
  • Playlists in and out in the common playlist format, so your work is not locked in.

Keeping it safe

  • Your files are never changed. Corrections you make stay in the app.
  • Works offline, in a hall with no signal.
  • Nothing is recorded or uploaded. No account, no analytics, no tracking.

Restore

Worn transfers, cleaned up

Clicks, crackle and hiss taken down: live while you play, or as new copies of your worst transfers.

Live, from the first note

Each recording gets only as much cleaning as it needs, and clean ones are left alone. A worn track is never let go halfway: on a dance floor, crackle coming back is worse than a little cleaning.

The Restore Workshop: new copies

For the worst transfers, drop in files or a whole folder ahead of the evening. Each is saved as a new lossless file, and the player plays the restored copy in place of the original. Your originals never change.

Questions

Good to know before you try it

Does it play Spotify or other streaming services?

No. Tanda & Tone plays your own music files: MP3, AAC and Apple Lossless, WAV, AIFF and FLAC.

Do I have to import or retag my library?

No. Point it at your music folders and it reads the files where they are. Where the tags are not tango-shaped it takes what it can from the artist and the title, and you can correct a track in the app. Your files are never changed.

Will it flatten the dynamics?

No. The big correction is between tracks, where the difference comes from the transfers and not from the music. Inside a track the touch is light: a soft passage comes up by a few dB, a build keeps its climb, a pause is never filled and the last chord lands at full weight. If you want less, turn Leveling towards Soft.

How is this different from normalising my files?

Normalising gives each file one level, and that is the first thing Tanda & Tone does too. It then does what one number per file cannot: a gentle ride inside the track so soft passages carry in a full room, cleanup of clicks and crackle, the same pause between tracks and a limiter for the PA. And nothing is written to your files.

Can I have less of it, or none?

Yes. Leveling goes from Soft to Strict, and Soft is the lightest touch. Quiet lift, under Engineer, sets how far a soft passage may come up, down to nothing. Restore can be switched off. Limiter only gives one track just its level and the limiter. Bass, Mid and Treble are yours, and so is your mixer.

What do I need for pre-listen?

Headphones on a device of their own: a USB headphone adapter or Bluetooth headphones. The Mac's own jack goes to the room.

What if the Mac stops in the middle of a milonga?

The room goes quiet, because Tanda & Tone is the player. Open it again and it offers to go on from the same track with the same Up Next. As with any setup, keep a reserve on its own mixer channel, such as a phone with a playlist.

What will it cost?

The price is not set yet. It will be free for 14 days and then one purchase, with no subscription.
