As recorded In the room

Tango lives on contrast: a whispered violin, a long build, a held pause before the last two chords. A full floor is not a quiet room, though. The softest passages sink under the talk and the shoes, and the loudest are what make someone ask you to turn it down. A small, slow correction keeps the whole piece inside what the room can carry.

On a worn transfer

Clicks, crackle and hiss, taken down with a light hand

While it plays

Each recording gets only as much cleaning as it needs, and a clean transfer is left alone. A worn track is never let go halfway: on a dance floor, crackle coming back is worse than a little cleaning. Off is one click.

Ahead of the night: the Restore Workshop

For your worst transfers, drop in files or a whole folder. Each is saved as a new lossless file, and the player plays the restored copy in place of the original. Your originals never change.

Your call

How it should sound is still your decision

Tanda & Tone takes the repair work: the jumps between transfers, the wear. What the room should sound like tonight is yours, and every control for it is one click away.

Leveling
How much the level is looked after. Soft is the lightest touch.
Quiet lift
How far a long, soft passage may come up. Zero switches that lift off.
Room, Bass, Mid, Treble
The tone for this hall, this PA and this orchestra.
Restore
Off, Auto or On. Auto decides for each track.
One track
A little higher or lower for a single track, remembered for its next play. Limiter only leaves a track with its level match and nothing more.
Volume
Your mixer's fader, as on any other night.

If you know your compressors. Tango DJs who write about sound tend to describe the same ideal: level the library first, then a soft, slow compressor that takes a few dB off the loudest passages and is almost unnoticeable, with the bass kept out of what it listens to. That is how Tanda & Tone is built. Each track is levelled before it plays, so little is left for the compressor to do; the double bass and the left hands do not make the music pump; and the limiter at the end only catches the peaks that would hurt the PA.

A night at the milonga

Made for the whole evening,
from the first tanda to La Cumparsita.

What Tanda & Tone does, in the order you meet it.

  1. 17:00At home

    It has heard every track before the floor does.

    Point it at your music folders. It reads orchestra, singer and rhythm from your tags, FLAC included, and listens through every track once, so each one's level is known before you play it.

    Search and filter by orchestra, singer, rhythm and year, or show only what you have not played for a month, and a tanda is a few clicks. Not sure who sings on a track? Right-click it to look the title up on tango.info or El Recodo in your browser.

    Where you own the same recording from several albums, it marks the transfer that sounds best. Which one plays is still your choice.

  2. 19:30Before the doors

    T T V T T M, laid out with its cortinas.

    Select four tracks and add them as a tanda, and a cortina follows it. Or lay out the whole night from a template and drop the tracks into its slots.

    A tanda you want again is saved with one click and waits in the sidebar for any evening.

    Up Next shows every tanda with its orchestra and the time it ends. A small mark hints when a tanda mixes orchestras, singers or rhythms. It is a hint, never a block.

    TTVTTM

    Tango, tango, vals, tango, tango, milonga, with a cortina after each.

  3. 20:00Doors open

    Set the mixer once.

    Every track comes out at the same level, so the gain you set on the first track holds for the night. After that, a move on the fader is a choice, not a repair.

    Then lock the player. Locked, Stop and Next need a short hold, so a stray click cuts nothing short.

  4. 20:30The first tanda

    The floor can read what is playing.

    Put a second window on the projector or a TV. It shows the orchestra, the title, the singer and the year in type that reads across the room, and where the tanda is.

    Your name and the milonga's stand at the top. What it says about the next tanda is yours to decide: its rhythm, its orchestra too, or nothing at all.

  5. 22:10A full floor

    Amber is the room. Blue is only your headphones.

    The bar along the top shows what the room hears: orchestra, singer and year, the time left in large digits, which tanda you are in and when it ends.

    Pre-listen plays any track in your headphones while the room plays on, at about the level the room will give it. Nothing blue ever reaches the room.

  6. 22:22The cortina

    The cortina knows its length.

    A cortina plays for the time you set, 30 seconds to start with, and fades out. Between two tracks the pause is the length you choose, whatever silence the files carry at their ends.

    And when the floor asks for it, Fade out ends a track or a cortina softly.

  7. 00:50The last tanda

    Quiet for the announcement, and tonight's set to share.

    End after this tanda lets the tanda play out and then holds the room quiet, for an announcement or a break.

    When the last chord has sounded, Export Tonight saves the evening as a text list and a playlist file, ready for the dancer who asks what that vals was.

Your library

Your library stays yours.

You do not import anything into a new format, and you do not retag. Tanda & Tone reads the music where it already is.

Read where it is

Tags are read from your files, FLAC included. Where they are not tango-shaped, it takes what it can from the artist and the title. A catalogue of the golden-age orchestras in the app gives a short name in full, and a singer or recording date your tags lack where it knows one for certain. You can correct any track in the app.

Never changed

Nothing is written to your files. Corrections you make stay in the app, and restored copies are new files.

Not locked in

Playlists go in and out in the common playlist format, so your work travels with you. Correct a file's tags in your own tagger whenever you like: the track keeps its playlists, its tandas and its history.

Private

It works offline, in a hall with no signal. Nothing is recorded or uploaded. No account, no analytics, no tracking.

Questions

Good to know
before you try it.

More in the support guide.

What do I need?

A Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 14.2 or later, your music as files, and a cable from the Mac's headphone jack to the mixer.

Does it play Spotify or other streaming services?

No. Tanda & Tone plays your own music files: MP3, AAC and Apple Lossless, WAV, AIFF and FLAC.

What happens to the dynamics?

They stay. The big correction is between tracks, where the difference comes from the transfers and not from the music. Inside a track the touch is light: a soft passage comes up by a few dB, a build keeps its climb, a pause is never filled and the last chords land at full weight. If you want less, turn Leveling towards Soft.

How is this different from normalising my files?

Normalising gives each file one level, and that is the first thing Tanda & Tone does too. It then does what one number per file cannot: a gentle ride inside the track so soft passages carry in a full room, cleanup of clicks and crackle, the same pause between tracks and a limiter for the PA. And nothing is written to your files.

Can I have less of it, or none?

Yes. Leveling sets how much, and Soft is the lightest touch. Quiet lift, under Engineer, sets how far a soft passage may come up, down to nothing. Restore can be switched off. Limiter only gives one track just its level and the limiter. Bass, Mid and Treble are yours, and so is your mixer.

What do I need for pre-listen?

Headphones on a device of their own: a USB headphone adapter or Bluetooth headphones. The Mac's own jack goes to the room.

What if the Mac stops in the middle of a milonga?

The room goes quiet, because Tanda & Tone is the player. Open it again and it offers to go on from the same track with the same Up Next. As with any setup, keep a reserve on its own mixer channel, such as a phone with a playlist.
